Losing Fat and Keeping It Off: A No-Hype Guide

Fat loss is the most searched, most over-promised topic in health. The honest version is simpler (and less profitable to sell) than the industry wants you to believe: it's about a sustainable, modest energy deficit you can actually live with — supported by protein, movement and sleep. Here's how to do it without the nonsense.

The honest truth about fat loss

Fat loss comes down to consuming a bit less energy than you burn, consistently, over time. Every diet that works — whatever it's called — works because it helps you do that. There's no metabolic trick that bypasses it, and crash diets usually backfire because they're impossible to sustain.

How to lose fat and keep it off

  • Eat enough protein. It keeps you full and protects muscle while you lose fat.
  • Build meals around whole foods and fibre — more filling per calorie, steadier energy.
  • Strength-train and stay active. This preserves muscle so most of what you lose is fat, and keeps the weight off long-term.
  • Sleep. Short sleep raises appetite and makes everything harder (our sleep guide).
  • Aim for slow and steady — roughly 0.5–1% of bodyweight a week is sustainable; faster usually isn't.

Your metabolism, realistically

Metabolism varies far less between people than the marketing suggests, and no food or supplement meaningfully "boosts" it. The two things that genuinely help are having more muscle (from strength training) and staying active day to day. Extreme dieting can lower your energy output, which is another reason to avoid crash approaches.

Supplements and the "fat burner" myth

Here's the blunt bit: "fat burner" supplements don't meaningfully work Overhyped, and some are risky. Caffeine gives a tiny, temporary bump; protein powder and fibre can help you stick to a deficit by keeping you full — that's about it. Save your money for good food.

A note on weight-loss medication

Prescription weight-loss medicines (such as the GLP-1 drugs) can be effective for some people, but they are prescription-only, need medical supervision, and are a decision for a doctor — not something to buy online. If your weight is affecting your health, your GP is the right place to discuss all the options safely.

When to get support

If you're struggling despite the basics, have a lot of weight to lose, or weight is affecting your health, ask your GP — there's genuine, judgement-free support available, from dietitian referrals to medical options.

Frequently asked questions

What's the best way to lose fat?

A sustainable, modest energy deficit supported by enough protein, whole foods and fibre, strength training to protect muscle, and good sleep. Slow and steady (about 0.5–1% of bodyweight a week) is what actually keeps the weight off.

Do fat burner supplements work?

Not meaningfully, and some are risky. Caffeine offers a tiny temporary bump, and protein or fibre can help you feel full, but there's no supplement that melts fat. Your money is better spent on good food.

Can I really boost my metabolism?

Not much — metabolism varies less between people than marketing suggests, and no food or supplement meaningfully boosts it. Building muscle and staying active are the only real levers; crash dieting can actually lower your energy output.

Should I consider weight-loss medication?

Prescription weight-loss medicines like GLP-1 drugs can help some people, but they're prescription-only and need medical supervision — a decision for your GP, not an online purchase. If weight is affecting your health, your GP can discuss all the options safely.
