Menopause and Perimenopause: Symptoms, HRT and Real Support

Menopause is a normal life stage, not a disease — but that doesn't mean you have to just grit your teeth through it. There are genuinely effective options, and a lot of unhelpful noise. Here's a clear, honest guide to what's happening and what actually helps.

What's actually happening

Perimenopause is the transition, when hormones (mainly oestrogen) fluctuate — it can start in your 40s and last several years, and periods become irregular. Menopause itself is diagnosed after 12 months without a period (on average around age 51 in the UK). After that is postmenopause.

Common symptoms

Everyone's experience differs, but common ones include hot flushes and night sweats, disrupted sleep, mood changes and anxiety, brain fog, joint aches, vaginal dryness, changes in libido, and — importantly for the long term — a faster loss of bone density.

HRT — the most effective option

Hormone replacement therapy (HRT) is the most effective treatment for menopausal symptoms, and it also helps protect bone health. The current consensus among UK and international bodies is that for most women starting it under 60 or within 10 years of menopause, the benefits outweigh the risks — the blanket fears from older headlines have been substantially revised. It isn't right for everyone, so it's a personalised conversation with your GP or a menopause specialist, who can weigh your own history and options.

Lifestyle that genuinely helps

  • Strength and weight-bearing exercise — protects bone and muscle, and helps mood and sleep.
  • Prioritising sleep and managing stress — both take a hit during this transition.
  • Limiting alcohol and caffeine, which can trigger hot flushes and worsen sleep for some.
  • A balanced diet with enough protein and calcium for muscle and bone.

Supplements — the honest evidence

Supplements can support the basics but aren't a substitute for medical treatment where that's needed:

  • Calcium and vitamin D Strong (for bone) — important for bone health, which matters more after menopause. Vitamin D is worth considering year-round for many in the UK.
  • Magnesium Promising — may help with sleep and relaxation.
  • Omega-3 Moderate — general heart and health support.
  • Phytoestrogens (soy isoflavones) and black cohosh Mixed — some women find them helpful for hot flushes, but the evidence is inconsistent and they aren't right for everyone (check with a clinician first, especially with a history of hormone-sensitive conditions or liver issues).

When to see your GP

See your GP if symptoms are affecting your quality of life, if you have questions about HRT, or for any unusual bleeding (for example bleeding after menopause), which should always be checked. Effective help is available — you don't have to simply endure it.

Frequently asked questions

What are the first signs of perimenopause?

Often irregular periods alongside hot flushes, night sweats, disrupted sleep, mood changes, brain fog and joint aches. It can start in your 40s and last several years as hormone levels fluctuate.

Is HRT safe?

For most women starting it under 60 or within 10 years of menopause, the current consensus is that the benefits outweigh the risks, and it's the most effective treatment for symptoms plus bone protection. It's a personalised decision — discuss your history with a GP or menopause specialist.

What supplements help with menopause?

Calcium and vitamin D matter for bone health after menopause; magnesium may help sleep, and omega-3 supports general health. Phytoestrogens and black cohosh help some women with hot flushes but the evidence is mixed — check with a clinician first.

When should I see a doctor about menopause?

See your GP if symptoms affect your quality of life, if you'd like to discuss HRT, or for any bleeding after menopause, which should always be checked.
