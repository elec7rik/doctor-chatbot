Gut Health Basics: What Actually Helps

By the My Longevity Hub editorial team · Updated September 2026 · 3 min read

Gut health has become a buzzword, but the basics are genuinely powerful and refreshingly simple. Your gut microbiome thrives on the same things the rest of you does — real food, movement, sleep and less stress. Here's what actually helps, and an honest note on the peptides people ask about.

The foundations of a healthy gut

  • Eat more plants, and more variety. Fibre and a wide range of plants (a common target is ~30 different plant foods a week) feed a diverse, resilient microbiome — the best-evidenced thing you can do.
  • Include fermented foods — yoghurt, kefir, sauerkraut, kimchi — which may support gut bacteria.
  • Go easy on ultra-processed foods and excess alcohol.
  • Manage stress and sleep — the gut and brain are closely linked, and stress genuinely affects digestion (see our sleep guide).
  • Move regularly and stay hydrated — both help digestion and regularity.

Probiotics and supplements

  • Probiotics Mixed — effects are strain- and situation-specific; some strains help some people (for example after antibiotics or with IBS), but they're not a universal fix.
  • Fibre / prebiotics Moderate — feed your existing good bacteria; food sources come first.
  • Peppermint oil Moderate — has evidence for IBS-type symptoms in some people.

The peptide research angle

One research peptide that comes up in gut conversations is KPV, a small anti-inflammatory peptide fragment that has drawn scientific interest for gut and inflammation pathways.

  • KPV Animal-only — interesting preclinical research on inflammation; human clinical evidence is lacking.
Important: KPV is sold for laboratory research use only — it is not a licensed medicine, not approved for human use, and this guide isn't advising anyone to take it. The foundations above are where real gut improvements come from.

Red flags: when to see a GP

See your GP for blood in your stool, unexplained weight loss, a persistent change in bowel habits, difficulty swallowing, or ongoing pain. These always deserve proper assessment rather than a supplement.

Frequently asked questions

How can I improve my gut health?

Eat more plants and more variety (a common target is around 30 different plant foods a week), include fermented foods, go easy on ultra-processed foods and alcohol, manage stress, sleep well and move regularly. Diet diversity is the best-evidenced lever.

Do probiotics actually work?

Sometimes — effects are strain- and situation-specific. Certain strains help certain people (for example after antibiotics or with IBS), but probiotics aren't a universal fix and food-based fibre and fermented foods come first.

Is KPV good for gut health?

KPV is a small anti-inflammatory peptide with interesting preclinical (animal) research on gut inflammation, but human clinical evidence is lacking. It's sold for laboratory research use only and isn't approved for human use.

When should I see a doctor about gut symptoms?

See a GP for blood in your stool, unexplained weight loss, a persistent change in bowel habits, difficulty swallowing, or ongoing pain — these need proper assessment, not a supplement.
