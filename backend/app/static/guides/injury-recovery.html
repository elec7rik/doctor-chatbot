Injury Recovery: How to Heal Faster

By the My Longevity Hub editorial team · Updated September 2026 · 3 min read

Whether it's a tweaked tendon, a strained muscle or a joint that won't settle, the honest truth about recovery is that time plus smart loading does most of the work — and a few basics genuinely speed it along. Here's what helps, plus a clear-eyed look at the peptides people ask about.

The foundations of healing

  • Relative rest, not total rest. Complete rest often weakens tissue; gentle, progressive movement within comfort usually helps most injuries heal stronger. A physiotherapist can guide the right loading.
  • Progressive loading and rehab. Tendons and muscles adapt to gradual, controlled stress — this is the single most effective driver of recovery for most soft-tissue injuries.
  • Sleep. Repair happens largely while you sleep; protect it (see our sleep guide).
  • Enough protein and overall nutrition. Healing tissue needs building blocks and energy — don't under-eat while injured.
  • Don't smoke, and keep alcohol modest — both slow healing.

Nutrition and supplements for recovery

  • Protein Strong — the raw material for repair; spread it across the day.
  • Vitamin C Moderate — needed for collagen synthesis.
  • Vitamin D Moderate — supports bone and muscle; worth not being deficient in.
  • Collagen peptides Promising — some evidence for tendon and joint support.
  • Omega-3 and creatine Promising — may support recovery and help maintain muscle while you're less active.

The peptide research frontier

Two research peptides come up constantly in recovery conversations: BPC-157 and TB-500. Both have generated real scientific interest for soft-tissue, tendon and wound-healing pathways — but it's important to be honest about where the evidence actually is.

  • BPC-157 Animal-only — striking results in animal studies; robust human clinical trials are still lacking.
  • TB-500 (thymosin beta-4) Animal-only — similar picture: interesting preclinical research, limited human data.
Important: these are sold for laboratory research use only — they are not licensed medicines, not approved for human use, and this guide is not telling anyone to take them. Treat them as science to read about, and rely on the proven foundations above to actually recover.

Common recovery mistakes

Resting too completely, returning to full load too soon, chasing passive treatments while skipping rehab exercises, and under-eating are the classics. Consistency with rehab beats any shortcut.

When to see a professional

See a physiotherapist or GP for pain that's severe, not improving after a couple of weeks, or accompanied by significant swelling, instability, numbness or inability to bear weight — getting the diagnosis and rehab right is what actually gets you back.

Frequently asked questions

How can I heal an injury faster?

Prioritise relative rest with gentle progressive loading (ideally guided by a physio), protect your sleep, eat enough protein, and don't smoke. These foundations do most of the work; rehab consistency beats any shortcut.

Does BPC-157 work for tendon injuries?

BPC-157 has generated real scientific interest for soft-tissue and tendon healing, but the strong results are largely from animal studies — robust human trials are still lacking. It's sold for laboratory research use only and is not approved for human use.

What supplements help recovery?

Enough protein is the big one, plus vitamin C (for collagen), vitamin D, and possibly collagen peptides, omega-3 and creatine. They support the foundations rather than replacing rehab.

When should I see a physio or doctor about an injury?

See a professional for severe pain, an injury not improving after a couple of weeks, or one with significant swelling, instability, numbness or inability to bear weight.
