Living Longer: The Habits That Actually Work

"How do I live longer?" is the question behind half of everything on this site. The honest, slightly boring answer: the biggest wins are the basics done consistently for decades. The exciting research frontier is real too — but it's the icing, not the cake. Here's how to think about both.

The levers that actually move the needle

Decades of population research point to the same short list. Get these right and you've done the vast majority of what's in your control:

  • Don't smoke — nothing else you do matters as much if you do.
  • Move, and keep your muscle. Regular activity plus strength training is one of the strongest predictors of a long, independent life. See our muscle guide.
  • Eat mostly whole foods — plenty of plants, enough protein, not too much ultra-processed food.
  • Protect your sleep. Chronically short sleep works against almost every longevity marker (our sleep guide).
  • Keep alcohol modest and manage stress.
  • Stay socially connected. Loneliness is a genuine health risk; relationships are a longevity factor, not a soft one.

Supplements with a longevity angle

No pill replaces the levers above, but a few have a reasonable evidence base for healthy ageing: vitamin D (especially in the UK), omega-3, and creatine (muscle and possibly brain). See our supplements guide.

NAD+ and cellular ageing

One of the most active areas in longevity science is NAD+, a coenzyme central to energy production and cellular repair that declines with age. Raising NAD+ with precursors has been shown to lift levels in humans, and research into what that means for healthy ageing is genuinely promising Promising — though it's an emerging field, not a proven fountain of youth. Worth understanding honestly rather than over-selling. Our energy guide covers the NAD+ story in more depth.

Cutting through the hype

Longevity marketing is full of miracle molecules and expensive tests. Be sceptical of anything promising to "reverse ageing" — the evidence for the basics is vastly stronger than for any supplement, and always will be. Do the boring things brilliantly first.

Frequently asked questions

What actually helps you live longer?

The strongest levers are not smoking, staying active and keeping muscle, eating mostly whole foods, protecting sleep, keeping alcohol modest, managing stress, and staying socially connected. These matter far more than any supplement.

Do longevity supplements work?

No supplement replaces the lifestyle basics, but vitamin D, omega-3 and creatine have reasonable evidence for healthy ageing. Be sceptical of anything promising to 'reverse ageing'.

Does NAD+ help with ageing?

NAD+ is a coenzyme central to energy and repair that declines with age. Precursors can raise NAD+ levels in humans and the healthy-ageing research is promising, but it's an emerging field rather than a proven anti-ageing cure.
