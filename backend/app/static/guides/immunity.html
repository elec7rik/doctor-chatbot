Supporting Your Immune System: What Actually Helps

By the My Longevity Hub editorial team · Updated September 2026 · 3 min read

You can't crank your immune system up like a dial — but you can support it to work as well as it should, and stop dragging it down. Here's the honest version.

Every winter the "immune boosting" products come out in force. The honest science is less dramatic but more useful: you can't crank your immune system up like a dial, but you can support it so it works as well as it should — and avoid the things that drag it down.

Can you really "boost" your immune system?

Not in the way the marketing implies. A healthy immune system isn't "low" and in need of boosting — it needs to be balanced and well-supported. The goal is to give it what it needs and remove what undermines it, not to super-charge it.

The foundations of a resilient immune system

  • Sleep. Consistently short sleep measurably weakens immune defences (our sleep guide).
  • A varied, mostly-plant diet. A wide range of nutrients and fibre supports both immunity and gut health (our gut guide).
  • Regular movement and not smoking.
  • Manage stress — chronic stress suppresses immune function.
  • Good hygiene and staying up to date with recommended vaccinations.

Supplements that genuinely support immunity

A few nutrients have authorised roles in normal immune function — most useful when you're actually short of them:

  • Vitamin D Moderate — contributes to normal immune function, and many people in the UK are low in winter; the NHS suggests considering 10 micrograms daily over the darker months.
  • Vitamin C Moderate — contributes to normal immune function; won't prevent colds but may slightly shorten them for some.
  • Zinc Moderate — contributes to normal immune function; useful mainly if you're low, and high doses aren't better.

What's overhyped

Mega-dose "immune boosters", most exotic mushroom blends and high-dose antioxidant stacks promise far more than the evidence supports — and very high doses of some nutrients can be counterproductive. The basics beat the hype.

When to see a doctor

See your GP if you get frequent, severe or unusually persistent infections, or infections that don't clear as expected — this can occasionally signal an underlying issue worth checking.

Frequently asked questions

Can you actually boost your immune system?

Not in the way marketing implies. A healthy immune system doesn't need boosting so much as balancing and supporting — giving it what it needs (sleep, good food, movement, low stress) and removing what undermines it.

What supplements help immunity?

Vitamin D, vitamin C and zinc all contribute to normal immune function, and are most useful if you're actually short of them. Vitamin D is worth considering in the UK winter. High doses aren't better and some can backfire.

Does vitamin C stop colds?

It won't prevent colds for most people, though it may slightly shorten them for some. It contributes to normal immune function as part of a good diet rather than acting as a cure.

Why do I keep getting sick?

Often it's down to run-down basics — poor sleep, high stress, a patchy diet. But frequent, severe or unusually persistent infections that don't clear as expected are worth seeing a GP about.
