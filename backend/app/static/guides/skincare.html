Skincare That Actually Works: An Evidence-Based Guide

By the My Longevity Hub editorial team · Updated September 2026 · 3 min read

Skincare is a huge, noisy market — but the truth is refreshingly boring: a handful of well-evidenced basics do almost all the work, and everything else is optional polish. Here's what genuinely moves the needle for healthy, resilient skin as you age, plus an honest look at where peptides fit.

The proven basics (start here)

  • Daily sunscreen Strong — the single most effective anti-ageing step there is. UV drives most visible ageing (lines, pigmentation, laxity). A broad-spectrum SPF 30+ every day beats any serum.
  • Retinoids (retinol / tretinoin) Strong — the gold standard for smoothing texture and softening fine lines. Over-the-counter retinol is milder; prescription tretinoin is stronger and, in the UK, comes from a doctor. Start low and slow to avoid irritation.
  • A gentle routine Strong — a mild cleanser and a good moisturiser protect the skin barrier. Over-exfoliating and harsh actives do more harm than good.
  • Vitamin C Moderate — a morning antioxidant serum can help with brightness and support collagen.
  • Niacinamide Moderate — well-tolerated, helps with barrier, redness and oil balance.
Lifestyle counts too: not smoking, decent sleep, and a balanced diet all show up on your face over the years.

Collagen and skin supplements

Collagen is the scaffolding that keeps skin firm, and it declines with age. Oral collagen peptides Promising have some evidence for improving skin elasticity and hydration in trials, which makes them one of the more defensible "beauty" supplements. Vitamin C also supports your own collagen production, and overall a good diet does more than any single capsule. See our supplements guide for how to choose well.

The peptide research frontier

Peptides are short chains of amino acids that can act as signals in the skin, and they're an active area of research. The best known is GHK-Cu (copper peptide), which has generated genuine scientific interest for its role in skin repair, collagen and wound-healing pathways. Some cosmetic peptides (like the "Matrixyl" family) also appear in topical products.

Important: research-grade GHK-Cu sold by peptide suppliers is for laboratory research use only — not a cosmetic to apply to your skin, and not for human use. If you're curious about the science, treat it as exactly that: research to read about, not a product to self-experiment with.

What's overhyped

Most 10-step routines, "miracle" creams and expensive essences add cost, not results. If a product promises to "erase" wrinkles overnight, it can't. Consistency with the basics beats novelty every time.

When to see a dermatologist

See a professional for persistent acne, rosacea, any new or changing mole, or a skin concern that's affecting your confidence — prescription options and expert guidance are worth it.

Frequently asked questions

What's the most important skincare step?

Daily broad-spectrum sunscreen (SPF 30+). UV exposure drives most visible skin ageing, so protecting against it outperforms any serum or cream.

Do collagen supplements work for skin?

There's promising evidence that oral collagen peptides can modestly improve skin elasticity and hydration. They're one of the more defensible beauty supplements, but a good diet and sun protection matter more.

Are copper peptides (GHK-Cu) good for skin?

GHK-Cu has genuine research interest for skin repair and collagen pathways. However, research-grade GHK-Cu sold by peptide suppliers is for laboratory research use only — not a cosmetic to apply and not for human use.

Do I need an expensive skincare routine?

No. A simple routine — sunscreen, a gentle cleanser and moisturiser, and a retinoid — covers the essentials. Most elaborate, pricey routines add cost rather than results.
