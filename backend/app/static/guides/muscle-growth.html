Building Muscle and Recovering from Training

Building muscle isn't complicated, but it is demanding: it comes down to progressively harder training, enough protein, and real recovery. No supplement or peptide out-runs those three. Here's the honest hierarchy of what matters — and where the peptides people ask about actually sit.

The foundations of building muscle

  • Progressive overload — gradually doing more over time (weight, reps, sets) is the core stimulus.
  • Enough protein — roughly 1.6–2.2 g per kg of bodyweight a day, spread across meals, is the well-supported range for building muscle.
  • Recovery and sleep — muscle is built between sessions, not during them; protect your sleep and don't train the same thing into the ground.
  • Consistency over months — the real "secret". Nothing beats showing up.

Supplements that actually help

  • Creatine monohydrate Strong — the most-proven supplement for strength and muscle, safe and inexpensive.
  • Protein powder Strong (as food) — not magic, just a convenient way to hit your protein target.
  • Caffeine Moderate — can improve training performance.
  • Vitamin D Moderate — worth not being deficient in for muscle function.

Growth-hormone peptides: the research picture

Peptides such as CJC-1295 and Ipamorelin are growth-hormone secretagogues — in research they prompt the body to release more of its own growth hormone. They're a genuine area of study, but the honest position is that human evidence for real-world muscle and body-composition benefits is limited, and manipulating growth hormone is a hormonal intervention with unknown long-term effects.

  • CJC-1295 / Ipamorelin Limited human data — increase GH/IGF-1 in studies; meaningful long-term outcome data in healthy adults is lacking.
Important: these are sold for laboratory research use only — not licensed medicines, not approved for human use, and this guide is not advising anyone to take them. Any hormonal manipulation is a medical matter. The proven foundations above are what actually build muscle.

What's overhyped

BCAAs (if your protein is already adequate), most "test boosters", and elaborate supplement stacks add cost more than muscle. Fundamentals first, always.

Training smart and staying safe

Build load gradually to avoid injury (see our recovery guide), and if you have a health condition or are new to training, get clearance and ideally coaching to start well.

Frequently asked questions

What's the best way to build muscle?

Progressive overload in your training, eating enough protein (about 1.6–2.2 g/kg/day), and getting real recovery and sleep — applied consistently over months. Everything else is a minor add-on.

Which supplements actually build muscle?

Creatine monohydrate has the strongest evidence and is safe and cheap; protein powder is a convenient way to hit your target; caffeine can help performance. Most other 'muscle' supplements are overhyped.

Do CJC-1295 and Ipamorelin build muscle?

They raise growth hormone in research, but human evidence for real muscle and body-composition benefits is limited, and manipulating growth hormone has unknown long-term effects. They're sold for laboratory research use only and aren't approved for human use.

How much protein do I need to build muscle?

Roughly 1.6–2.2 grams per kilogram of bodyweight per day, spread across meals, is the well-supported range for building muscle alongside progressive training.
